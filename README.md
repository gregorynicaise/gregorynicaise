### 🧬 About

French research scientist specialised in experimental and computational science applied to nuclear safety analysis. Chemical engineering degree, MSc and PhD in materials science, statistical physics and quantum mechanics (Université de Lille / CNRS, 1998–2001) — doctoral work yielded a landmark result: the first isolation of the physical conditions governing liquid metal embrittlement of nuclear steels by lead, combining fracture mechanics experiments with ab initio DFT computations, in the context of Generation IV lead-cooled fast reactor development.

Currently serving as **Scientific Advisor at the ASNR Nuclear Emergency Centre**, leading the development and implementation of advanced methods and fast computational tools for real-time predictive evaluation of accident source terms, and heading the emergency source term response team.

A career that spans the full breadth of nuclear safety science — from reactors to radwaste — with a sustained focus on fission product behaviour across their various physical states, especially under accidental conditions. This encompasses reactor-scale accident physics and chemistry, the development of advanced source term evaluation methods, and nuclear safety analysis of next-generation reactor concepts covering sodium-cooled, lead-cooled, gas-cooled, molten salt, and supercritical water designs.
